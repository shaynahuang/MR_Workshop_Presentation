01 · Definition

What exactly
do we manipulate?

The field extends beyond grabbing and moving a hologram to changing an object’s spatial state, intrinsic structure, and relationship with the physical world.

Manipulation is not a gesture. It is a complete chain from selection and transformation to confirmation.

04 · Interaction loop

Four gates in
every manipulation

01

Acquire

How is the target discovered and selected? Near grabs, rays, gaze, and proxies all begin here.

02

Map

How do body and device motion map onto the object—1:1, gain-adjusted, non-linear, or frame-transformed?

03

Constrain

Free 6/9-DOF, or axis, plane, snap, and collision constraints? Fluidity and precision are balanced here.

04

Confirm

When does the object release, settle, or revert? Visual, audio, haptic, and ghost-pose feedback close the loop.

06 · Lineage

A lineage of
MR manipulation

1995

WIM

Shrinks an entire world into the hand; establishes exocentric proxy manipulation.

1996

Go-Go

Extends the virtual arm non-linearly, taking direct grasp beyond physical reach.

1997

HOMER

Transitions from ray selection to hand-centered control for hybrid remote interaction.

1999

Voodoo Dolls

Uses transient bimanual proxies to expose scale and reference frames.

2000

Magic Paddle

A physical paddle pushes, carries, and manipulates virtual objects in tangible AR.

2012

Handle Bar

Two hands grip an invisible bar to integrate position, rotation, and scale.

2019

ARPen

A phone supplies the view while a pen supplies precise bimanual modeling input.

2020

Tangible Proxy

A generic sphere balances passive haptics with reuse across virtual forms.

2024–25

Reality Proxy

Proxies expand from virtual objects to distant and occluded physical objects.

10 · Synthesis

Six claims from
the literature map

01

Recombination outweighs invention

Most MR systems recombine known metaphors: hand rays blend ray casting, HOMER, and virtual-hand control; Bounds Control spatializes the 3D gizmo.

02

Remote bare-hand control remains open

Weak depth control, absent contact force, tracking noise, arm fatigue, and DOF cross-talk still occur together.

03

Rigid transformation dominates

Translation and rotation are universal; scale is less consistent; deformation, topology, grouping, and semantic change remain underexplored in head-worn MR.

04

Tangibles work—but cost

Physical proxies improve stability and touch while adding prop proliferation, tracking, and deployment costs. Generic proxies seek a compromise.

05

Results resist comparison

Studies vary in distance, size, DOF, device, constraints, and measures. Completion times alone are rarely comparable.

06

Physical objects are the new frontier

Reality Proxy, HoloBots, and MR drag-and-drop shift the problem from moving holograms to acting on physical objects through virtual representations.
